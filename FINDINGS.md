To estimate the cost of a ~$200 stablecoin transfer from Korean Won (KRW) to Vietnamese Dong (VND), there are three distinct steps: USDT-KRW exchange, the on-chain transfer fee, and USDT-VND conversion. My first step consisted of converting KRW to USDT through the KuCoin peer-to-peer (P2P) marketplace. I attempted to use Binance P2P and OKX P2P, the two most commonly referenced platforms, but neither displayed KRW/USDT pairs at the time of data collection. To estimate the spread, I averaged the top buy prices on March 27, 2025 that gave a figure of approximately 1,587.44 KRW per USDT, compared to a mid-market rate of approximately 1,507 KRW per USD sourced from Google Finance on the same date. This implies a spread of approximately 5.3%, or around $10.60 on a $200 transfer. Adding the 0.1% fee from KuCoin, the total cost is then $11.74. The assumption here is that the sender already holds a verified KuCoin account and also a Korean bank account. A new user might face verification delays.

Once the sender holds USDT, they send it directly to the recipient's wallet over the Tron blockchain (TRC-20). At current prices, sending USDT to an existing wallet costs approximately $1.54 in TRX, which is around 0.77% of a $200 transfer. This is the one component that clearly delivers on the stablecoin promise as it is fast, cheap, and transparent.

The final step was converting USDT into VND. On Binance P2P on March 27, 2025, merchants were offering approximately 28,100 VND per USDT, above the Google Finance mid-market rate of 26,337 VND per USD on the same date. Mechanically, this would imply a negative spread of −6.7%, meaning the recipient would receive more VND than the official rate suggests. For the purposes of this estimate, I treat this off-ramp spread as approximately 0%, reflecting the assumption that the P2P price and the true market rate are roughly aligned. One significant assumption here is that Vietnam's State Bank prohibits crypto as a means of payment, but P2P trading operates in a legal grey zone and is widely practiced. The recipient is assumed to have a Vietnamese bank account and a verified Binance account. 

It is also worth noting that this three-step process requires accounts on two separate platforms: KuCoin for the on-ramp in Korea and Binance for the off-ramp in Vietnam. Each requires identity verification. This friction cost does not appear in any fee estimate but it can be a real barrier, particularly for first-time users.

My best estimate for a $200 stablecoin transfer from South Korea to Vietnam is $13.30, or 6.22% of the transfer amount. This comprises a KRW/USDT on-ramp spread of 5.34% ($10.68) observed on KuCoin P2P, a 0.1% KuCoin platform fee ($1.07 — note that Binance charges 0% but did not list KRW pairs at the time of data collection), and a TRC-20 on-chain fee of $1.56 (0.78%). The off-ramp, converting USDT to VND in Vietnam, was treated as zero cost. 

Against the RPW corridor average of 5.15% ($10.30) for Q3 2025, the stablecoin route is more expensive by approximately $3.00. Against the cheapest available traditional option, Woori Bank via internet banking at 2.01% ($4.02), the stablecoin route is more expensive by $9.28. The on-chain fee of $1.56 is genuinely the cheapest component in the entire comparison, cheaper than any provider in the RPW database. However, the fiat conversion costs on both ends more than erase that advantage. The stablecoin route does not simply fail to compete with the best option, it also fails to beat the corridor average. For a sender with internet banking access, the traditional channel is both cheaper and simpler. That said, the comparison depends heavily on who the sender is. A worker using a cash agent by default, would save meaningfully by switching to stablecoins. The problem is that those senders are also the least likely to have verified exchange accounts, crypto wallets, and the digital literacy the stablecoin route requires.

The three cost components above were observable from public data. KuCoin P2P prices are browsable without an account, TRC-20 fees are documented on gasfeesnow.com and tronscan.org, and RPW corridor data is publicly available.

Several components could not be measured. The most significant practical obstacle was that KRW/USDT trading was unavailable on both Binance P2P and OKX P2P at the time of data collection. If the two largest P2P platforms do not list KRW pairs, the on-ramp is less accessible than the stablecoin remittance literature typically assumes, and the observed KuCoin spread may not be representative of what a sender would actually encounter. South Korea's crypto regulatory environment adds further complexity: domestic exchanges like Upbit operate under the Virtual Asset User Protection Act and do not easily support outbound USDT transfers for remittance purposes, meaning a sender must navigate offshore platforms with accounts that may require non-Korean documentation. 

The off-ramp produced an unexpected result. Binance P2P merchants in Vietnam were offering 28,100 VND per USDT, above Google Finance's mid-market rate of 26,337 VND per USD. Rather than indicating a benefit, this reflects the gap between Vietnam's official managed exchange rate and the true market-clearing rate for dollars, which is structurally higher due to persistent USD demand. I treated the off-ramp cost as zero on this basis, but I could not obtain a verified parallel market rate to calculate the true spread precisely. This is a genuine data gap.
Other unmeasured costs include KYC setup time across two separate platforms (KuCoin for the on-ramp, Binance for the off-ramp), counterparty risk on P2P trades, spread variability across time and merchants, and the regulatory risk inherent in Vietnam's grey-zone crypto market.

Two data sources could improve this analysis. First, household surveys of Vietnamese migrant workers in Korea that ask how they currently remit, what they pay, and whether they have used or attempted crypto, would reveal whether the theoretical cost comparison reflects actual behavior. Second, State Bank of Vietnam remittance statistics disaggregated by channel would help identify whether stablecoin flows are showing up as unaccounted inflows. Vietnam publishes aggregate remittance data but does not break it down by instrument. Collaboration with the State Bank, potentially through World Bank country office channels, would be the most realistic path to obtaining this.
